International Trade Commission OIG
Fiscal Year 2026 Financial Statement Opinion
Type:
Audit
View Report
1

Establish and implement written procedures and responsibilities for identifying and approving capitalizable costs, determining and documenting placed-in-service dates, and communicating and recording asset additions, transfers, disposals, and reclassifications in accordance with applicable federal accounting standards.

2

Assess personnel’s understanding of capitalization, placed-in-service reporting, and depreciation requirements and provide targeted training where gaps are identified.

3

Establish, document, and implement periodic reconciliation processes of project cost records, asset inventories, the fixed asset subsidiary ledger, and the general ledger, including supervisory review and timely investigation and resolution of identified differences.

4

Conduct and document a risk-based review of existing PP&E and internal use software balances, including software under development to verify capitalization decisions, asset classification, placed-in-service dates, useful lives, and related depreciation and amortization. Identify, validate, and record any additional needed corrections in the appropriate reporting periods and disclosures in accordance with applicable federal accounting standards.

5

Identify the underlying root causes of the identified deficiencies and implement a corrective action plan that establishes accountable officials and completion dates and includes procedures for monitoring corrective actions through completion.

6

Establish a process for assessing and documenting the operating effectiveness of corrective controls, including follow-up on identified deficiencies, to provide reasonable assurance that the controls are functioning as intended and that financial reporting errors are prevented or detected and corrected timely.

7

Establish and implement documented procedures for evaluating the significance of accounting errors and the severity of related internal control deficiencies to include distinguishing financial statement materiality assessments from material weakness determinations and separately evaluating the likelihood and potential magnitude of misstatements arising from identified control deficiencies and relevant mitigating controls.
