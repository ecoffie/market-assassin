## 1. LinkedIn — GovCon Giants page

> **What does the public record show about a $210 million acquisition?**
>
> On January 22, 2026, Tetra Tech announced it had acquired Halvik, a federal data-analytics and IT modernization contractor. Tetra Tech's later filings put the fair value of the purchase price at approximately $210 million.
>
> The Mindy Institute reconstructed Halvik's federal prime-contract record as it stood the day before that announcement:
>
> → **245** prime awards and contract vehicles
> → **$724.7M** in cumulative public federal obligations since FY2014
> → **$3.1M** of obligations in FY2017 → **$186.7M** in FY2025
> → **92.6%** of those obligations were associated with awards recorded under small-business, 8(a) or WOSB set-aside classifications
> → **4 departments** account for 94.1%
>
> The study also checks Halvik's public claims against the record. All 17 vehicles it named are in the federal record. NASA's largest award reconciles within 0.03%. Three dollar claims remain unresolved, and the study says so.
>
> The study is just as clear about what the record can't establish: revenue, backlog, margins, or why the buyer paid what it paid.
>
> Read the full study, with its method and every source, at The Mindy Institute: getmindy.ai/research/halvik-tetra-tech

## 2. LinkedIn — Eric

> Every small business I talk to asks some version of the same question: what does a federal contracting business actually look like by the time someone wants to buy it?
>
> So the Mindy Institute took one real deal and rebuilt it from the public record.
>
> Halvik. Acquired by Tetra Tech in January 2026. Tetra Tech's filings put the price at approximately $210 million.
>
> The federal record, as of the day before the deal was announced:
>
> → 245 prime awards and vehicles
> → $724.7M in public federal obligations, starting in 2014
> → FY2017: $3.1M. FY2025: $186.7M.
> → 92.6% of those obligations tied to awards recorded under small-business, 8(a) or WOSB set-asides
>
> The part I'd underline: most of that money didn't come through one big contract. It came through **vehicles**: OASIS, STARS, ITES-3S and agency BPAs that Halvik won and then kept winning orders on. Orders under the five largest carried 78% of the total.
>
> Nobody hands you the customer. But the government publishes, in detail, how the customer was won.
>
> The study is careful about what this does and doesn't prove. These are obligations, not revenue. Ceilings, not backlog. It's one company, not a rule.
>
> The full study is at The Mindy Institute: getmindy.ai/research/halvik-tetra-tech

## 5. Podcast discussion outline

**Segment:** "Reading an acquisition from the public record"

1. **Set-up (host, 1 min):** what the study is and isn't. Obligations versus revenue; ceilings versus backlog.
2. **Prompt to a guest who has sold or bought a GovCon firm:** "When a buyer looked at your contract list, what did they ask about first: concentration, recompetes or set-asides?"
3. **Vehicles versus contracts:** orders under Halvik's five largest vehicles carried 78% of obligations. Is the vehicle the asset, or is it the relationship behind it?
4. **What the public record misses:** revenue recognition, subcontract work, CPARS and pipeline. Which of these mattered most in a deal you've seen?
5. **Set-asides and ownership change:** keep it to "these are instruments that warrant transaction-specific review." No predictions about eligibility. Point listeners to counsel and the regulation, not to us.
6. **Close:** link to the study at The Mindy Institute.

**Guardrail for hosts and guests:** guests interpret findings; they are never the source of a number. Don't make claims about Halvik's 8(a) dates, revenue, or what happens to its contracts.
