# Market Report Section: How They Buy Now (template)

Owner: Eric (standard, 2026-11-27). Encoded for Ask Mindy in `src/lib/ask-mindy/how-they-buy-now.ts` (research mode and
`create_document` guidance). `[KB n]` = claim id `KB-n` in the rules register (`kb_rule_claims`, Data Core #2518), read
through its lookup (`searchRuleClaims` / `checkClaims`). A report cites a rule claim only when the register returns it as
current; when the register is unavailable or the claim is not current, the claim is left out. Never cite a rule from a
doc file or from memory. Client examples are described generically here; this repository is public.

It goes into every Mindy market or company report and leave-behind. Arc: the old way is closing, what this firm holds,
the eight pathways, talent, attention, Monday actions.

## Rules for writing the section

1. Lead with the client's buyers, not the rules. Every rule mentioned says what it means for THIS client.
2. Each door is fit, possible, or not applicable.
3. Cite every claim (memo, EO, deviation, statute, and Mindy data).
4. Never promise awards, revenue or dates. Use "We get [client] in front of the buyers...".
5. Established contractors get the paths, not opportunity lists. Newcomers get paths plus one concrete first target.
6. No em dashes, plain language, acronyms explained on first use.

## Block 1. How the government buys now (every report)

Two to four Summary bullets, chosen from these five shifts by what touches the client. Each names the shift, cites it,
and says what it means here.

| Shift | When it applies | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| The rulebook was rewritten (agencies follow the rewritten FAR through class deviations; formal rules still proposed) | always, in one line | EO 14275 [KB 8] |
| Fewer automatic set-asides (no order of precedence; order-level set-asides are discretionary) | any small business, especially one leaning on a certification | RFO Part 19 deviation [KB 1, 3] |
| Commercial first (non-commercial buys need written justification) | clients selling standard products or services | EO 14271; FY26 NDAA Sec. 1822 [KB 9] |
| Common buying moves to GSA | IT, professional services, facilities, common categories | EO 14240; OMB M-25-31 [KB 10] |
| The Department of War rebuilt how it buys (Portfolio Acquisition Executives own capability areas) | any DoW buyer; Block 2 is then required | Nov 7 2025 memo; FY26 NDAA Sec. 1802 [KB 11] |

Example (a spectrum-planning firm): "The Department of War rebuilt how it buys. Program Executive Officers are becoming
Portfolio Acquisition Executives (PAEs): one accountable leader per capability area, with a senior contracting official
embedded to award quickly."

## Block 2. The new DoW acquisition structure (required for any DoW buyer)

1. One paragraph on what changed: the Nov 2025 Warfighting Acquisition System memo; PAEs own a capability end to end,
   contracting sits in the same office, "held solely accountable for outcomes", speed, commercial preferred, a two-year
   transition.
2. A table: Service | Status | the portfolio that owns this client's work. Name a portfolio and leader only where a
   source confirms them.
3. "Why this is better for [client]": one leader instead of layers of program offices, contracting in the same office,
   and a CSO lets them propose against a stated problem.
4. When PAEs do not own the client's work, say so. Example (a digital advertising agency): "Recruiting advertising is
   bought by recruiting commands, National Guard state offices and base contracting squadrons, not by the
   weapons-focused PAEs. Spend your time on those offices."

### Consortium decision (every report; one line in the report)

1. Does the client build or prototype something? Service firms (staffing, janitorial, advertising, consulting) do not
   join a consortium; say so and point to their real doors.
2. Is there a portfolio or command that buys that capability? Match it to the focus table below.
3. Is the consortium still issuing project calls in that area? Check its site and recent awards (a quiet consortium is a
   fee with no return).
4. Prime or team? A firm new to DoW usually teams with members who already win, and joins later.
5. Construction firms use the military construction OTA authority (below), not a technology consortium, unless they
   bring a new construction method.

One-line format: "Join [consortium] (sponsor: [command]) because [capability match]; first move: [team with X / answer the
next project call]" OR "No consortium: [reason]; your doors are [Y]."

Examples: a spectrum-planning firm: National Spectrum Consortium or S2MARTS. A healthcare staffing firm: none (MTEC buys
medical technology, not staffing). A finished-software firm: none; use a CSO or Tradewinds. A construction firm: none;
watch the military construction OTA authority.

Membership is through the consortium's management firm; check the fee on its own site and never quote a fee the site
does not confirm. Members get white-paper calls that lead to prototype awards with possible follow-on [KB 13].

### Construction OTA

The FY2026 NDAA created 10 U.S.C. 2808a, letting the military departments use OTAs for "repair and construction projects
for facilities". Not limited to new technology, no express dollar cap; success can lead to follow-on without competition;
Congress is notified 14 days before award; FAR construction protections (changes, differing site conditions, bonds) are
negotiated, not assumed; no DoW implementing guidance as of July 2026. Use it for every construction client with a DoW
buyer. Source: Holland & Knight, Jul 23 2026:
https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/the-new-ota-authority-for-military-construction

### After the doors

- TALENT TEST: "Can a contracting officer verify [client]'s record in four minutes?" (public awards, SAM codes, SBA
  profile, written past performance).
- ATTENTION: where they are known, and which buyers still need an introduction.

## Block 4. Where the money is moving (every report)

A From | To | What it means table, two to five client-tied rows. Examples: PEOs to PAEs with contracting built in (fewer
doors, CSOs as a fast route); agency consulting and IT contracts to GSA Schedule and governmentwide vehicles (OMB M-25-31;
a GSA vehicle becomes the default); large general consulting to work tied to a named mission problem (lead with the
mission problems they solve); from 8(a) to HUBZone and small business (a HUBZone certification becomes the strongest door).

## Block 5. Outreach in a less set-aside world (every report) = the action plan

Five moves, filled with named offices and people:

1. Build the buyer list: buying offices, not agencies; target 25; include ordering offices on vehicles held and the owning
   PAE portfolio. Sample rows: office and why.
2. Brief the right offices: small business offices are the front door; office, contact (verified only), the introduction
   to ask for.
3. Call the right people: named contracting officers from live notices, PAE small business leads, primes' supplier
   liaisons; who, why, the exact question.
4. Say what buyers need to hear: capability and proof win the set-aside decision; three pitch lines tied to cited buyer
   problems, plus "What are you planning in the next 12 months? Will it be set aside, and for whom? Which vehicle will
   you use? Who else should we talk to?" (outreach rules: buyer first, certification last).
5. Work the right events: matchmaking and industry days where those buyers are; named events with dates, verified only;
   if none are confirmed, say so.

Rules for less set-aside protection (pick what fits, name partners): show up in market research; team with firms that
already win (incumbents, vehicle holders, veteran-owned firms at the VA [KB 4], mentor-protege); get the GSA vehicle
first; price and describe the work as commercial; ask how each buy will be structured (set-aside type, vehicle, timing;
at the VA always ask veteran-owned vs small business generally).

## Where the blocks go in the report

1 Summary (Block 1 bullets) · 2 Where [client] stands · 3 What the buyers are dealing with (GAO/IG) · 4 New DoW structure
(Block 2, if a DoW buyer) · 5 Where the money is moving (Block 4) · 6 Pathways and examples (Block 3, with opportunities
and recompetes as supporting examples) · 7 Readiness checklist (always including SBA profile keywords) · 8 Action plan
(Block 5) · 9 How GovCon Giants helps · 10 Mindy box and sources.

## Ship checklist

- Each Block 1 shift is cited, with what it means for this client.
- Block 2 is present for any DoW buyer, or says PAEs do not own their work.
- All 8 doors are marked.
- The talent test and attention are filled in.
- The money table has client-specific lines.
- The action plan names real offices and people from Mindy or official pages; no invented contacts.
- Unverified items are said plainly; no promises of awards, revenue or dates.

## Hardening (Eric, 2026-11-27)

- Fix 2, boilerplate drift: the swap test (swap in another client's name; any sentence still true is rewritten to name
  this client's buyer, codes and situation, or cut). "Not applicable" always carries a one-sentence client-tied reason;
  never mark a door not applicable because prime history is thin (check subcontracts, JVs, mentor-protege, commercial and
  state work first). When length is a problem, cut from the top: Blocks 1 and 4 (briefing) go before Blocks 3 and 5
  (decisions).
- Fix 3, contacts (Eric: no dates for contacts either; no provenance clutter): every named contact carries a citation
  marker [n] pointing to its source (the SAM notice number and office, or the official page) in Sources. No "from the
  notice..." prose and no dates in the table or body. Staleness is checked internally: a contact whose newest source is
  older than 180 days is dropped or marked "confirm this contact before reaching out". No contact is reused from an
  earlier report without a re-check. Fewer verified contacts beat a long list. No named contact: say so and give the
  office's published inbox. Never infer an email from a name pattern.
- Fix 1 (Data Core): the source register and a stale-claim check called before a report ships.
- Fix 4: a decision log at ship time (design: `tasks/report-decision-log-design-2026-11-27.md`; no migration until Eric
